# Solution research

Systematically search for a strong solution to the supplied problem or decision without claiming literal exhaustiveness or objective ideality. Establish the evaluation foundation before generating candidates, preserve the evidence trail, make uncertainty visible, and publish only conclusions supported by the run.

## Inputs, precedence, and invariants

Accept the canonical invocation from `/research-best-solution`:

```text
problem_or_decision: optional text
max_review_rounds: 1 | 2 | 3 (default 3)
active_context: conversation context plus relevant repository/runtime evidence
```

1. Apply the command's exact cap grammar. Treat problem text as opaque except for the exact final cap token; do not reinterpret malformed cap tokens.
2. The following precedence is mandatory: hard constraints and the evidence-sufficiency gate; the frozen frame revision; criterion definitions, metrics, weights, and tie-breakers; uncertainty-aware ranking; soft preferences. No estimate, agent agreement, convenience, or later artifact may override an earlier level.
3. Before proposing solutions, explain in plain language what is being solved, why it matters, the desired outcome, the decision owner, and the solution goals and non-goals. Use that context to derive the selection rubric rather than starting from generic criteria.
4. Build a candidate criterion pool by mapping every proposed criterion to at least one goal, hard constraint, stakeholder outcome, high-severity failure mode, lifecycle cost, or validation need. Consider outcome correctness, implementation cost and time, simplicity, operability and maintenance, security and privacy, performance, reversibility, evidence availability, and opportunity cost only when relevant. Reject irrelevant, duplicate, inseparable, or easily gamed proxy criteria and record why.
5. Classify every retained criterion as a hard constraint, weighted criterion, or ordered tie-breaker. Define its metric, unit or observable state, preferred direction, acceptance threshold or target, measurement method, evaluation horizon, and what failure means. Every weighted criterion must have executable `0..100` normalization anchors: quantitative criteria define lower/upper anchors and interpolation or threshold bands; qualitative criteria define ordered behavioral anchors with observable evidence. Metrics must measure the intended outcome rather than convenient activity.
6. Assign non-hard weights totalling 100 using explicit swing-weight reasoning: estimate the decision value of moving each criterion from its worst acceptable result to its best plausible result, grounded in goal importance, failure consequence, and stakeholder impact. Record the rationale and uncertainty. If the decision owner supplies no weights, derive provisional weights instead of distributing them equally; mark them low-confidence, ask the single focused clarification when weight ambiguity could change the winner, and make the recommendation conditional when sensitivity analysis still changes the winner.
7. Prevent double counting. Identify criteria sharing the same outcome, evidence, causal mechanism, or cost and either merge them, establish non-overlapping measurement boundaries, or reduce and justify their combined weight.
8. A material correction to the problem, goals, non-goals, hard constraints, criteria, metrics, weights, tie-breakers, evaluation method, complexity classification, quorum trigger definitions, or evidence policy creates a new dated frame revision. Invalidate and completely re-evaluate and re-rank all downstream candidates, evidence ledgers, scores, and critiques against that revision. Never mix scores or findings from different revisions. A frozen downstream quorum trigger becoming active from observed candidate or sensitivity results is a gate outcome, not a frame correction.
9. Keep assumptions distinct from evidence, decisions, risks, and unknowns. Do not fabricate source URLs, runtime observations, agent outputs, completed checkpoints, provenance, or evidence sufficiency.

## Deterministic paths, bounded identity, and collision safety

1. Derive a filesystem-safe `topic-id` from a lowercase Unicode-normalized title: replace non-alphanumeric runs with `-`, trim `-`, and use `problem` if empty. Retain at most 48 ASCII slug characters and append `-` plus the first 12 hexadecimal characters of a digest of the normalized full title. The ID must never contain raw problem text beyond that bound, secrets, or sensitive text.
Use the same parent-derived grammar for filesystem identity components: `task-<slug>-<digest12>`, `slice-<slug>-<digest12>`, and `candidate-<slug>-<digest12>`. Normalize the descriptive label with Unicode NFKD, remove combining marks, lowercase it, replace non-ASCII-alphanumeric runs with `-`, trim `-`, retain at most 32 ASCII slug characters, and use `item` if empty. `digest12` is the first 12 lowercase hexadecimal characters of SHA-256 over the complete canonical identity, not merely the visible label. Each component is at most 64 ASCII bytes. The parent derives and reserves task/slice IDs before dispatch and candidate IDs before candidate-path creation; workers return the assigned IDs and never choose path components from prose.
2. Use `ai_docs/artifacts/research/<topic-id>/` for every intermediate and subagent artifact. Use `ai_docs/research/<topic-id>.md` for the final solution and `ai_docs/artifacts/research/<topic-id>/execution-report.md` for the run report.
3. Before any artifact write, make one durable atomic create-if-absent claim at `ai_docs/artifacts/research/.claims/<claimed-topic-id>/` (or the runtime's equivalent atomic claim primitive). The successful claim is the sole reservation for both `ai_docs/artifacts/research/<claimed-topic-id>/` and `ai_docs/research/<claimed-topic-id>.md`; record the claim path and both exact reserved paths in the claim/recovery record. On claim collision, retry the same atomic claim with `-YYYYMMDDTHHMMSSZ`, then `-1`, `-2`, and so on, until claimed; never choose a run by checking whether a path exists. Bound collision retries at 20; on exhaustion, mark the run `BLOCKED` without writing a competing run.
4. A claimed run may create its artifact directory and write every intermediate, subagent, and execution-report file only below that directory. The claim namespace is control metadata, not an intermediate artifact. The exact final publication path is the sole permitted path outside the artifact directory and may be created only by a create-if-absent publication of the exact `final-solution.md` content; if that final path already exists unexpectedly, mark the run `BLOCKED` and never overwrite it. The claim, claimed ID, artifact directory, execution-report path, and final publication path are authoritative for the run and for collision recovery. Never overwrite a prior run silently.
5. Use Markdown only. Required artifact names and schemas are frozen below; add files only when a phase needs them.

### Frozen artifact schemas

Every artifact begins with a small metadata block containing `artifact_type`, `topic_id`, `frame_revision`, `created_at_utc`, `producer_role`, and `source_refs`. A worker-returned artifact body MUST itself satisfy the applicable frozen Markdown schema, including that ordinary metadata block; authoritative runtime identity is never worker-authored metadata.

Each worker payload that reaches `payload-validated` MUST pass destination preflight before materialization: verify the reserved task/slice/path identity, reject duplicate or superseded assignments, and inspect any existing destination and manifest record for an identity or canonical-content conflict. Only a collision-free create-if-absent destination, or an idempotent existing destination with byte-identical canonical content and matching identity, may advance to materialization. The artifact bytes are exactly the UTF-8 bytes of the returned `markdown_body`; no additional bytes or transformations are permitted. After the atomic write or byte-identical no-op, verify the destination bytes and manifest record; only then may the result enter `accepted` and become semantic evidence. Invocation identity, resolved model/provider, materializer identity, reserved path, canonical byte length, and canonical content SHA-256 are recorded only in the `execution-report.md` manifest. Parent-authored syntheses cite immutable worker artifact paths; canonical candidate synthesis is separately parent-authored.

## Finite run budget and saturation

Freeze the applicable defaults in `execution-report.md` before Phase 2 or `STANDARD_FAST_PATH` validation; a stricter user limit wins. They are maxima, not targets. Every path debits the total-launch budget. On `STANDARD_FAST_PATH`, record the validation wave as consumed and mark only the inapplicable taxonomy, breadth, DFS, sensitivity, quorum, and critique budgets `NOT_REQUIRED_STANDARD_FAST_PATH`.

| Resource | Maximum per run |
| --- | ---: |
| Criteria-discovery waves | 1 |
| Breadth-first waves | 3 |
| Depth-first waves | 3 |
| Total subagent launches, including retries and critics | 46 |
| Replacement/retry launches | 2 |
| Candidate families receiving full DFS | 5 |
| Distinct evidence probes | 20 |
| Hybrid candidates attempted | 2 |
| Selection-quorum waves | 1 |
| Critique/revision rounds | `max_review_rounds` (at most 3) |

Before any high-severity coverage attempt or any launch, retry, evidence probe, DFS analysis, hybrid attempt, or critique round, check every hard maximum that the work would consume. Start the work only when every applicable budget has remaining capacity, and debit each applicable budget when the attempt begins. If any applicable maximum is exhausted, perform no further work that consumes it: stop the affected activity before saturation, set the stop reason to `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`, record every not-yet-attempted high-severity item blocked by that exhaustion as a coverage gap, record the required resource and likely ranking effect, and reduce confidence. Record consumed and remaining amounts for every budget; no later instruction authorizes work after a hard maximum is exhausted.

Breadth saturation may stop further breadth work only after two consecutive completed breadth waves add no viable family or materially new high-severity evidence, every high-severity item has been attempted while its applicable budgets remained, and surviving families have received comparable counterevidence effort. If a budget exhausts before all of those conditions hold, the stop is `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`, not saturation. Never evade a budget through relabeling retries, probes, hybrid work, or other resource use.
## Coverage-driven selection, routing, provenance, and independence
Use one common coverage-selection policy for every agent-backed phase: criteria discovery, compact validation, breadth, DFS, selection quorum, critique, re-review, and any permitted replacement.

1. Before criteria discovery, build a provisional domain inventory from the problem context, goals, hard constraints, likely failure modes, lifecycle boundaries, required validation, and locally visible evidence. Do not begin with named roles or a fixed agent pool. Mark each inventory item `material`, `not-material`, or `unknown`. An item is `material` only when it can affect admissibility, ranking, a high-severity risk, or required validation; `not-material` items need no task; `unknown` items remain candidates for bounded discovery. Reconcile the inventory and statuses at frame freeze, after the full-path taxonomy, and whenever candidate mechanisms or evidence probes reveal a new boundary or collapse an assumed one. Record each reconciliation and its effect on assignments.
2. Define the live roster as the currently dispatchable agent definitions. Trigger examples in this skill are advisory signals, never an allowlist. For every shortlisted definition, check positive scope, exclusions, applicable phase, prerequisites, permissions, and native output compatibility. A definition failing any check is ineligible for that task. Recompute the roster at each wave; do not preserve a fixed eligible-agent pool.
3. Select the smallest justified task set that satisfies all material obligations and the phase's independence requirements. Primary discovery/DFS ownership is disjoint; quorum, critique, and re-review may intentionally overlap the same versioned artifact only when the tasks use distinct independence groups and the phase requires multiple independent assessments. Task count is not a cosmetic workflow quota and is never increased to shape the roster, fill concurrency, or create perspective symmetry. A stricter user maximum and every frozen run budget remain binding hard maxima, not prohibited workflow quotas. Do not omit a material obligation merely to fit a roster shape. Preserve one Task call for all prerequisite-ready independent work; runtime concurrency schedules that set, and this skill imposes no workflow-specific concurrency ceiling.
4. Before every proposed dispatch, load and apply `skill://reviewed-execution-task-escalation`. That skill alone classifies the task and resolves model routing; do not duplicate, weaken, or override its classification or routing rules here. The detailed selection, transport, materialization, and result contract is [agent-routing-and-result-contract.md](references/agent-routing-and-result-contract.md).
5. Before Task, resolve only fields designated pre-dispatch by the routing skill or runtime: classification, exact dispatch selector, selection reason, shortlisted execution-agent definition, permissions, prerequisites, and other explicitly defined pre-dispatch fields. Record the bounded task contract and stable task/slice IDs separately. Never require invocation ID or authoritative resolved model/provider identity before dispatch. If a mandatory pre-dispatch field is unavailable, record the exact routing block in `execution-report.md` and do not spawn or include that task. After dispatch, obtain and verify invocation ID and authoritative resolved identity from the spawned job record; missing, unexpected, or mismatched mandatory identity is `ENVIRONMENT_FAILED`, and the result is not semantic evidence.
6. Essential worker-result rule: first verify runtime identity, then validate that the payload's `markdown_body` is present, conforms to the assigned frozen schema, includes the ordinary artifact metadata block, identifies the stable task/slice assignment, and satisfies the bounded brief without self-review or unseen-peer claims. Before any write, compute the canonical byte length and hash and preflight the reserved task/slice/path plus existing destination and manifest state. Reject unresolved identity, stale-frame, assignment, duplication, reservation, path, or different-content conflicts without writing or overwriting anything. If preflight permits a collision-free create-if-absent write, materialize the validated UTF-8 `markdown_body` bytes exactly and record the write in the manifest; if the matching destination already contains byte-identical canonical content, treat materialization as an idempotent no-op and reconcile the manifest. Verify the destination bytes and manifest after that write or no-op. Mark the result `accepted` only after this verification succeeds. Invalid, incomplete, duplicate, unassigned, conflicting, or non-materialized payloads do not count as returned analysis or coverage and remain explicit gaps. Runtime identity, resolved model/provider, materializer identity, reserved path, and content hash belong only in the `execution-report.md` manifest.
7. Track three separate states for every material obligation: `assigned` (a permitted task contract exists), `accepted returned analysis` (a valid worker payload was received, materialized, verified, and accepted), and `evidence sufficiency` (the accepted worker artifact and other evidence support the required claim or gate). Assignment never counts as returned analysis; returned analysis never proves sufficiency. Parent work cannot discharge mandatory independent validation, DFS, selection-quorum, critique, or re-review requirements.
8. Define each selected task with one distinct material domain or slice, frozen frame revision, output schema, source paths, blindness requirements, and prohibition on self-review. Breadth primary ownership MUST be non-overlapping. DFS primary slices MUST be distinct, with overlap allowed only for targeted decisive claims. Quorum and critique deliberately assess common versioned artifacts; the semantic author of a claim cannot independently assess that same claim. Preserve phase-specific blindness, inputs, schemas, and pass conditions.
9. Record provenance for every material claim: producing role and stable worker artifact path; for agent-produced claims, resolved model/provider identity from the job record; source URLs or repository/runtime references; access date for external sources; and search/retrieval path. Assign an independence group to claims sharing a model/provider, source, search path, prompt lineage, or upstream artifact. Repeated agreement within one group is correlated corroboration, not independent evidence; blind tasks reduce anchoring but do not establish independence.
10. Keep breadth tasks blind to peer hypotheses, candidate rankings, and recommendations. While budgets remain, give surviving candidate families comparable disconfirming-evidence effort; if exhaustion prevents parity, record the shortfall as a coverage gap and reduce confidence. Never let an author critique the artifact it produced. If suitable agents are unavailable for a required wave, record the limitation and do not claim the checkpoint passed; continue only as an explicitly marked reduced-confidence run when routing and budgets permit, otherwise publish a blocked execution report and no final solution.

## Phase 0 — Initialize and clarify

1. Resolve the invocation and current context. Apply the command's exact cap validation.
2. Determine whether the supplied problem is sufficient. Ask at most one focused clarification when missing success criteria, hard constraints, or evaluation would materially change the answer. If no answer is available, state the ambiguity as an assumption or stop as `BLOCKED` when proceeding would be misleading.
3. Atomically create the durable claim in the claim namespace before creating the claimed artifact directory or execution-report skeleton. Record start time, invocation, context sources, claim path, both exact reserved paths, frame revision placeholder, tool/agent limits, and frozen budget.

**Checkpoint:** a unique durable claim reserves both the claimed artifact directory and exact final publication path; the problem is actionable or explicitly blocked; the cap is an integer from 1 to 3; no prior final was overwritten.

## Phase 1 — Establish and freeze the evaluation foundation

Write `selection-rubric.md` before `problem-frame.md`:

1. Start with a concise context brief explaining what is being solved, why it matters, the intended outcome, the decision owner, the decision horizon, and explicit goals and non-goals.
2. Derive a broad criterion pool from the goals, hard constraints, affected stakeholders, taxonomy-relevant risks already evident from context, implementation and lifecycle economics, and required correctness evidence. Map each retained criterion to its decision purpose; record rejected criteria and why they are irrelevant, duplicative, inseparable, or misleading.
3. Define validation metrics for each goal, hard constraint, and weighted criterion. Specify the observable, unit or state, direction, baseline when available, threshold or target, test or measurement procedure, representative data or scenario, time window, and stop or rollback condition. For every weighted criterion, define executable `0..100` normalization anchors and how evidence uncertainty becomes a score interval. Identify proxy-gaming, false-positive, false-negative, and measurement-validity risks where material.
4. Classify criteria as hard constraints, weighted criteria, or tie-breakers. Use swing weighting for non-hard criteria, document the rationale and uncertainty, total weights to 100, and resolve double counting before freezing. Treat owner-supplied weights as evidence to challenge, not unquestionable truth when they conflict with stated goals or hard constraints.
5. Define the simplest viable baseline appropriate to the problem: no change, stop doing the work, a manual procedure, existing native capability, configuration change, deterministic query or script, or the smallest local code change. Omit inapplicable forms, but never omit the simplicity challenge itself.
6. Classify the decision initially as `STANDARD` or `COMPLEX_SELECTION` using only context-observable scope and admissible-solution boundaries. Use `COMPLEX_SELECTION` when the problem necessarily or plausibly permits a viable solution crossing an irreversible or persistent-data boundary, security or permission boundary, production or external side-effect boundary, multiple-component or multi-owner boundary, or the frozen high lifecycle-cost threshold. Define that threshold in `selection-rubric.md` with an exact unit and value over the decision horizon, or mark it inapplicable with rationale. Record which scope triggers apply. Also freeze the one-way downstream quorum triggers: the actual provisional winner crossing any listed boundary or lifecycle-cost threshold, or the declared sensitivity matrix changing the winner or creating an unresolved first-place tie. Activation of a downstream trigger cannot revert to not required.

Before criteria discovery, complete the provisional domain inventory and mark every item `material`, `not-material`, or `unknown` under the common coverage-selection policy. Criteria discovery may refine the inventory but cannot replace it. Use repository/runtime evidence first. When criterion definitions, weights, or metrics depend on specialized domain facts unavailable locally, use current primary or official sources. For `COMPLEX_SELECTION`, or whenever criterion choice itself could plausibly change the winner, run a blind criteria-discovery wave over the material or unresolved domains required by the frozen frame. Keep tasks blind to candidate solutions, rankings, and peer drafts. Advance each payload through identity verification and schema validation, materialize the validated bytes exactly at `criteria/<stable-task-id>/<stable-slice-id>.md`, and count it as accepted only after the essential worker-result rule completes; parent-authored `selection-rubric.md` and `problem-frame.md` cite those immutable worker artifacts. For a simple, well-evidenced problem, record why criteria discovery is unnecessary and why each omitted domain is `not-material`.
Reconcile domain statuses and assignments at frame freeze; a materiality change that affects goals, constraints, criteria, metrics, weights, evidence policy, complexity, or quorum triggers creates the required new frame revision and invalidates downstream work.

Then write `problem-frame.md` with the testable decision sentence, context brief, final goals and non-goals, affected users and owner, measurable acceptance criteria, hard constraints, weighted criteria totalling 100, evaluation signals, fixed tie-breaker order, evidence policy, assumptions, observed evidence, and questions that would change the ranking. Cite `selection-rubric.md` and any criteria artifacts.

Freeze only after every retained criterion can be evaluated, every weight has a rationale, every goal or hard constraint has a validation metric, double counting is resolved, and the simplest viable baseline is defined. Mark the frame `BLOCKED` rather than inventing a criterion or metric. Record the frame revision hash/path in the execution report.

**Checkpoint:** the rubric is problem-specific, evidence-backed, measurable, non-duplicative, simplicity-aware, and frozen; every later candidate, ledger entry, score, quorum assessment, and critique cites the same frame revision.

### `STANDARD_FAST_PATH` gate

Immediately after frame freeze, the parent may propose a compact path only when all of these conditions are evidenced in `selection-rubric.md`: the initial classification is `STANDARD`; the simplest viable baseline directly satisfies every hard constraint and goal threshold; the supporting evidence is sufficient, current, and local; no high-severity unknown remains; no external source or specialist evidence is required to establish the frame; and a bounded parent alternative scan across no change, stopping the work, manual procedure, existing native capability, configuration change, deterministic query/script, and smallest local code change finds no materially distinct admissible mechanism that could plausibly change the decision. Record each considered form and its rejection or equivalence rationale.

Before accepting the compact path, reconcile the frozen material-domain inventory and select the smallest justified set of currently dispatchable definitions needed for independent validation. Check each shortlisted definition's positive scope, exclusions, phase, prerequisites, permissions, and native output compatibility. Trigger examples are advisory only. Preserve the compact path's inputs, blindness, schema, and pass condition: give each selected validator the frozen `problem-frame.md`, `selection-rubric.md`, local evidence references, and bounded alternative scan, but not peer outputs or a claimed final recommendation. Route and verify every dispatch under the common coverage-selection policy. Advance each worker payload through the essential worker-result rule at `fast-path-validation/<stable-task-id>/<stable-slice-id>.md`. The validation passes only when every material obligation has an accepted independent artifact and evidence sufficiency is established; assignment alone is not a pass.

When the gate and validation wave pass, write compact `comparison.md`, `final-solution.md`, and `execution-report.md`; record assignment coverage, accepted returned-analysis coverage, evidence sufficiency, invocation/resolved identity, worker artifact and parent synthesis paths/hashes, findings, and synthesized status. Runtime/materializer/hash manifest data belongs only in `execution-report.md`. Mark taxonomy, coverage map, breadth, agent DFS, selection quorum, sensitivity matrix, and critique as `NOT_REQUIRED_STANDARD_FAST_PATH`, then continue directly to Phase 7. The compact comparison must still apply hard constraints, the simplest-baseline challenge, evidence sufficiency, validation metrics, and the frozen tie-breakers. It may claim the recorded validation, but must not claim top-five breadth, full-path selection quorum or critique, or exhaustive search.
**Checkpoint:** the fast path is auditable from frozen local evidence plus complete independent validation artifacts covering all material domains; it removes only full-path work that cannot plausibly alter the decision. Otherwise continue to Phase 2.

## Phase 2 — Build edge-case taxonomy and coverage map

Create `taxonomy.md` before proposing solutions. Cover normal, boundary, adversarial, failure, migration/recovery, performance, security/privacy, operability, and maintenance conditions relevant to the frame. Do not force irrelevant categories. Reconcile the domain inventory after the full-path taxonomy: promote newly material or previously unknown domains, mark disproven domains `not-material`, and record the effect on coverage assignments. Create `coverage-map.md` mapping every taxonomy item to a domain assignment, expected evidence, and current coverage state.

**Checkpoint:** no high-severity taxonomy item is unassigned; all high-severity items have an attempted coverage plan; gaps are visible rather than silently omitted; assignment coverage is distinct from accepted returned-analysis coverage and evidence sufficiency.

## Phase 3 — Breadth-first blind hypothesis generation

Partition the coverage map into non-overlapping primary domain briefs. For each permitted wave, apply the common coverage-selection policy to the current material inventory and live roster; do not preserve a fixed pool or seek a prescribed perspective bundle. Fully define each bounded task, including its assigned domain/slice, frozen frame revision, output schema, source paths, and blindness requirements, then run all prerequisite-ready independent tasks in one Task call. Breadth primary ownership is non-overlapping; any overlap must be a targeted decisive-claim check, not duplicate ownership. Require each accepted worker artifact at `hypotheses/wave-<n>/<stable-task-id>/<stable-slice-id>.md` to identify its task and slice, frame revision, mechanism, expected fit, disconfirming evidence, references, and confidence. Do not add tasks for headcount or omit material obligations to fit a roster shape.

Run no more than the frozen breadth-wave, launch, retry, and evidence-probe budgets. Require the breadth set to test the simplest viable baseline and at least one materially different mechanism when viable. Explicitly ask whether no change, stopping the work, a manual procedure, an existing native capability, a configuration change, or a deterministic script can satisfy the goals before admitting a governance-heavy, permission-heavy, multi-component, or custom workflow candidate. Cluster only after breadth completion or an explicit budget/saturation stop. Create `clusters.md`, deduplicate semantically equivalent ideas, and retain a distinction only when mechanism, constraint fit, risk profile, or evidence materially differs. Produce the smallest genuinely distinct viable candidate-family set justified by material obligations; publish fewer when evidence supports fewer, and never pad or omit for roster shape.

**Checkpoint:** every taxonomy assignment has an accepted returned artifact, a recorded failure, or a `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` coverage gap; assignment, returned-analysis acceptance, and evidence sufficiency are separately recorded; hypotheses are blind at generation time; clustering rationale is auditable; saturation or budget stopping is explicit.

## Phase 4 — Depth-first candidate analysis and evidence ledger

Select no more than five viable candidate families for full DFS. For each, the parent creates `candidates/<candidate-id>.md` as a canonical synthesis citing immutable DFS worker artifacts and analyzes the mechanism against every frozen criterion and taxonomy item, including implementation/operation implications, dependencies, migration and rollback, failure modes, security/privacy, performance, maintainability, reversibility, and falsifiers. Prefer repository/runtime evidence.

Use external research only when the decision depends on facts unavailable locally. Consult current primary or official sources first; cite the exact URL, publication/update date when available, claim supported, access date, and retrieval/search path. Mark secondary or stale sources. Do not treat uncited assertions as evidence. Use no more than the frozen evidence-probe budget.

Create and maintain `evidence-ledger.md`. For each candidate/criterion, link support and counterevidence, assess directness, freshness, quality, independence group, and unresolved gaps. While applicable budgets remain, give every surviving family comparable effort to seek disconfirming evidence; record any budget-blocked shortfall as a coverage gap. 2. **Weight scenarios:** for each non-hard criterion `k`, run exactly two one-at-a-time scenarios from the baseline weights. Set `requested_target_k = min(100, weight_k + 10)` for the increase scenario and `requested_target_k = max(0, weight_k - 10)` for the decrease scenario. Let `requested_delta = requested_target_k - weight_k`, let `O` be the other non-hard criteria, and let `S` be the sum of their baseline weights. When `requested_delta > 0`, set `delta = min(requested_delta, S)` so no other weight can become negative; when `requested_delta <= 0`, set `delta = requested_delta`. If `O` is non-empty and `S > 0`, set `target_k = weight_k + delta` and each other weight `j` to `weight_j - delta * (weight_j / S)`. Hold hard-criterion weights and all other inputs at baseline, then rerank. Use exact arithmetic for ranking; if displayed rounding leaves a remainder, assign it in ascending criterion-ID order so displayed weights total 100. Record both the requested and applied target when feasibility caps the increase.
3. **Impossible proportional redistribution:** when `O` is non-empty, `S = 0`, and `delta < 0`, set `target_k = weight_k + delta` and distribute `-delta` equally across `O`. ## Failure handling

- **Insufficient context:** ask the single allowed focused clarification; if still material, mark `BLOCKED` or carry a plainly labeled assumption and lower confidence.
- **Rubric insufficiency:** if criteria cannot be mapped to goals, risks, or validation needs; metrics or normalization cannot establish correctness; weights lack a defensible rationale; material double counting remains; the lifecycle-cost threshold is undefined when applicable; or the simplest viable baseline is undefined, do not generate or rank candidates. Ask the single allowed focused clarification or mark the frame `BLOCKED`.
- **Agent routing or dispatch failure:** record the proposed task, live-roster checks, routing result, failed wave, role/domain/slice, error, mandatory field or identity block, and missing assignment/returned-analysis/evidence coverage. Do not spawn when any mandatory pre-dispatch field required by the routing skill or runtime is unresolved; invocation ID and authoritative resolved model/provider identity are not pre-dispatch fields and must never be required before Task. After dispatch, bind the invocation ID and authoritative resolved identity from the spawned job record; if either mandatory value is absent, unexpected, or mismatched, mark `ENVIRONMENT_FAILED`, do not use the result as semantic evidence, and follow the routing skill's reconciliation rules. Attempt a replacement only within the retry, launch, and wave budgets and only after the live-roster checks pass.
- **Invalid or missing worker result:** a missing `markdown_body`, invalid frozen Markdown schema, missing ordinary metadata block, wrong task/slice identity, duplicate assignment, self-review, or failed exact materialization is not accepted returned analysis. Do not count it toward coverage; preserve the failure and leave the material obligation as a gap. Runtime identity/materializer/hash details belong only in the `execution-report.md` manifest. Reconcile materiality when the failure reveals a boundary.
- **Budget exhaustion:** immediately stop work that consumes the exhausted resource and label the stop `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`. Record consumed and remaining budgets, all completed high-severity coverage attempts, every unattempted high-severity item as a coverage gap, work not performed, and the resulting lower confidence. Do not claim saturation unless its stated rule was met, and do not perform compensating work beyond another hard maximum.
- **Partial, correlated, or contradictory evidence:** retain all relevant sources, provenance, and conflicts; discount correlated agreement, widen uncertainty, and add a validation action. Never resolve by majority vote without independent evidence.
- **Evidence insufficiency:** every unresolved ledger gap marked `could_change_winner = true` forces a conditional recommendation with reduced confidence and a decisive validation action, regardless of criterion weight; if that action is unavailable, mark the run `BLOCKED`.
- **Candidate insufficiency:** publish fewer than five only when clustering and evidence show fewer genuinely distinct viable families; explain why. Unique accepted materialized worker artifacts, not assignments or invalid payloads, are the coverage basis, and parent-authored canonical candidate syntheses must cite them.
- **Critic failure or self-review risk:** preserve any original critic artifact as immutable and do not let the parent or semantic author masquerade as independent review. Replace the critic with the smallest justified independent permitted role before counting the round, but only while every applicable hard budget remains and all live-roster, routing, identity, and independence checks pass. Critics emit only open findings with `finding_status: UNRESOLVED`; the parent alone records `REJECTED_WITH_EVIDENCE`, `FIXED`, `ACCEPTED_RISK`, continuing `UNRESOLVED`, and closure. If replacement or mandated Critical/High re-review is blocked, mark the critique checkpoint incomplete and do not claim a passing review.
- **Selection-quorum failure:** when the initial classification is `COMPLEX_SELECTION`, when the actual provisional winner from the augmented pool crosses a frozen complexity boundary or lifecycle-cost threshold, or when the declared sensitivity matrix changes the winner or creates an unresolved first-place tie, quorum is mandatory; apply the common coverage-selection policy and treat missing, invalid, insufficient, or conflicting assessor worker artifacts as missing evidence, never as agreement. Issue decisive validation actions and a conditional recommendation, or mark the run `BLOCKED` when safe selection requires the checkpoint and no substitute evidence is available.
- **Write or path collision failure:** on a claim collision, use only the bounded suffix retries; on an exact final-path collision or failed create-if-absent publication, stop publication, preserve the authoritative execution report and any successfully written artifacts, mark the run `BLOCKED`, and report the exact blocker. Never overwrite or fabricate a path.
- **External-source unavailability:** continue with local evidence only when the decision can still be evaluated; otherwise mark the affected criterion unknown and reduce confidence, issue a conditional recommendation, or block under the sufficiency gate.
